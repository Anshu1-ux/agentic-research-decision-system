Cost comparisons between traditional data warehouses and lakehouse architectures
vary significantly by workload. Data warehouses typically charge for both storage
and compute together, often at premium rates, while lakehouses decouple storage
(cheap object storage like S3) from compute, letting organizations scale each
independently.

For read-heavy analytics workloads with irregular usage patterns, a lakehouse can
reduce total cost of ownership by 30-50% compared to a traditional warehouse,
mainly because idle compute isn't billed the same way. However, for small,
consistently-loaded workloads, a traditional warehouse's simpler pricing model
can sometimes be cheaper due to lakehouse operational overhead (compaction,
file management, cataloging).
